Where porches fail

A porch is a wood floor built outdoors. In the Pineywoods, where Texas Parks and Wildlife describes rain spread through the whole year and humidity typically high, the wood never gets a long dry season to recover.

The damage follows the water. Floorboards rot from their ends, where blown rain and splash soak into the end grain. Post and column bases rot where they sit on the floor or a slab and water collects around them; Fine Homebuilding and Woodworker's Journal both point to the bottom of the post as where rot usually starts. Steps rot where treads meet stringers. Railings loosen as the wood around their fasteners softens.

Older houses in Kilgore, Longview, Gladewater, Marshall and Henderson often still have their original porch framing. It is usually worth keeping, and fixing the parts that have failed costs far less than rebuilding the porch.

Safe checks before you call

You can learn a lot about a porch by looking and pressing, without any tools beyond a screwdriver.

  • Press a screwdriver into the floorboards near the edge and at the bottom of each post. Wood that is soft or spongy has started to rot.
  • Look where the porch meets the house for gaps, stains or soft wood.
  • Check for paint peeling at board ends and post bases, the first sign water is getting in.
  • Push on the railings and walk the steps.

Leave these to us: anything holding up the porch roof, a floor that sags or slopes, or framing under the porch. Do not jack, prop or cut a post yourself; the roof may be resting on it. Structural porch work can also need a city permit; the City of Kilgore asks homeowners to check with it before changing a structure.

Common questions

Can a rotted porch post be repaired instead of replaced?

Sometimes. When rot is limited to the bottom of a post, the damaged section can often be cut out and replaced and the base detailed so water drains away. If the post carries a roof load and the rot runs high, the post is replaced, with the roof supported while it is done.

Why do porch floorboards rot at the ends?

The ends of the boards soak up water through their end grain, and porch edges get rain blown in and splash from the ground. Paint and caulk failing at the ends lets water in. Replacing the full length of a damaged board usually lasts longer than patching the last few feet.

Is a sagging porch something I can check myself?

You can look for it, but do not try to jack or prop it. A porch that sags, slopes toward the house or moves underfoot has a problem in its framing or its footings, and the roof above may depend on it. Keep weight off it and call.

Do you repair porch steps and railings?

Yes. Worn or rotten treads, loose stringers, and wobbly railings and balusters are all part of the porch work we take on.

How much does porch repair cost?

It depends on what is damaged and how far the rot has gone, which is often more than shows on the surface. We look first and then quote.

A phone picture is plenty. You will hear what the piece needs, what it will cost and roughly how long it will take before anything starts.
