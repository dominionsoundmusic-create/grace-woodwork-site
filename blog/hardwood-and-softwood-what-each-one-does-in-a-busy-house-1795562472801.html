Hardwood and Softwood: What Each One Does in a Busy House

What hardwood and softwood really mean, and how each behaves in an East Texas home when it comes to dents, stain, movement and bugs.

The words hardwood and softwood sound like a hardness rating. They are not. They are botanical labels. Hardwoods come from flowering, broadleaf trees that carry seeds inside a fruit or nut, like oak, pecan, maple and walnut. Softwoods come from cone bearing trees with needles, like the pines and cypress that grow all over this part of Texas. The USDA Forest Products Laboratory treats the two words as the common names for those two plant groups, nothing more. That is why balsa, one of the softest woods on earth, is technically a hardwood.

So the label on a furniture tag tells you less than you would like. Here is what actually matters once a piece is in your house.

The number that predicts dents

The Janka test measures the force needed to press a steel ball halfway into a board, and it lines up with real life better than the hardwood label does. Eastern white pine comes in around 380 pounds force. Southern yellow pine, the pine of our woods, runs roughly 690 to 870. Cherry and black walnut land near 950 to 1,010, red and white oak near 1,290 to 1,360, hard maple around 1,450, and hickory around 1,820.

What that means at the kitchen table: a white pine top will record a dropped fork, a pressed pencil point and every homework session. Some families love that. Others find it maddening by year two. Southern yellow pine is a good deal tougher, and testing has shown it to be the strongest of the softwoods for impact and surface hardness, but it still bruises more readily than oak or hickory. Match the wood to the traffic. Hickory or maple for a daily eating surface, pine for a side table or a painted piece where marks read as character.

Grain structure changes how it finishes

Cut across the end of a board and you can often tell what you have. Oak, ash and elm are ring porous, with rows of large open pores that stay visible after sanding. If you want a smooth, glassy topcoat on oak, those pores have to be filled first or the finish will dip into every one of them. Cherry, maple, birch and poplar are diffuse porous, with a fine even texture that takes a film finish flat with no filler.

Softwoods have no vessels at all. Instead they have soft spring growth alternating with dense summer growth, which is why pine drinks stain unevenly and goes blotchy. Finish manufacturers address this with a pre stain conditioner, recommended specifically for soft or porous woods such as pine, fir, maple and cherry. If you are planning to restain a pine dresser, test your color on the back or underside first. You will learn more in ten minutes than in an hour of reading.

Humidity moves every bit of it

Wood is always trading moisture with the air around it until it settles at an equilibrium moisture content. At about 30 percent relative humidity that is roughly 6 percent moisture content; at 75 percent it climbs toward 14 percent. East Texas sits on the humid end most of the year and stays remarkably steady about it. Forest Products Laboratory data for outdoor exposure in Houston shows the smallest month to month swing of any station they measured.

Indoors is where the swings happen, between summer air conditioning and winter heat. Shrinkage is uneven by direction: tangential movement is about twice radial, which is why flat sawn boards cup and widen more than quarter sawn ones from the same log. Furniture conservators aim for relative humidity in the 40 to 60 percent range with slow changes rather than sudden ones. In practice, keep your thermostat and humidity reasonably steady, keep furniture off floor vents and away from sun baked exterior walls, and expect drawers to run tight in August and loose in January.

One more difference worth knowing. Lyctid powderpost beetles attack hardwoods almost exclusively, especially the sapwood of oak, ash, hickory and walnut, and they lay eggs on bare unfinished wood. A sealed or finished underside and back is real protection. Watch for pinholes with fine, flour like powder sifting out.
