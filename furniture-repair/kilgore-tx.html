Furniture Repair in Kilgore, Texas

Kilgore grew on the East Texas Oil Boom, and the derricks still standing downtown are the reason the town looks the way it does. The shop is here, which is the short version of why Kilgore jobs turn around faster than anything else — you can bring a piece by rather than describe it down the phone. Housing here runs to a mix of 1930s and 40s boom-era houses, mid-century brick ranches and newer builds out toward the highway, and what comes through the door tends to be oak and walnut pieces that came into families during the boom years and have been handed down twice since.

This is home. The shop is here, so a Kilgore job means no drive time and a quicker turnaround than anywhere else on this list.

What furniture repair covers

What that involves: loose joints, broken chair legs, drawers that stick, veneer lifting at the edges, water rings and heat marks. Repair is almost always cheaper than replacing, and on anything built before about 1980 the piece you own is better made than what you would buy to replace it.

Why furniture round here needs the work

Wood moves with the seasons here more than people expect. Drawers that stick in summer and rattle in winter are doing exactly what wood does; the fix is usually adjustment rather than replacement.

How to start

Start with a photo on your phone. That is usually enough to say whether something is a straightforward fix or a bigger job, and roughly what it runs. Nothing gets started before you know the number.

Questions from Kilgore customers

Can I bring the piece to the shop?

Yes — the shop is here in Kilgore, so you can drop in rather than describe the problem over the phone.

How much will it cost?

It depends entirely on the piece, which is why nothing is quoted sight unseen. Send a photo and you will get a real number before any work starts.

How long does it take?

Depends on the job and what is already in the shop. You will be told a realistic time up front, not an optimistic one.

Do not wait on it

A loose joint gets worse every time the piece is used, and a lifted edge of veneer catches on something and tears further. The repairs that get expensive are almost always the ones that were small a year ago.

Refinishing is not always the answer

Stripping a piece back is sometimes exactly wrong. An original finish, even a tired one, is part of what an older piece is worth, and once it is off it does not come back. Cleaning and reviving what is there is often the better job and the cheaper one.

Pieces made to order

A fair bit of the work here is a piece somebody wants built for a particular room or a particular person — a table sized to a space, a sign for a family place. Bring a measurement and an idea and it can be drawn up from there.
