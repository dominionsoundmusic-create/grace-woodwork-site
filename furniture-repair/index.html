What furniture repair covers in our shop

Furniture repair is the structural half of the work: making a piece solid and usable again. Furniture restoration adds the finish and the care an older piece deserves. We do both, in our shop in Kilgore, with three or four people at the benches.

Many jobs are wood furniture repairs that start small and get worse with use. A chair rocks because a glued joint has let go. A drawer drops because its runner is worn through. A table top has a white cloud where a hot dish sat. A strip of veneer on a dresser has lifted and caught on a sleeve. None of these is unusual, and none of them means the piece is finished.

How we approach a repair is simple. A loose joint gets taken apart, cleaned of old glue and reglued, not filled with screws. Missing trim gets cut to match. A finish that can be cleaned and revived is left on the piece rather than stripped. When a full refinish is the right call, it is done properly.

Problems we fix

Taken apart, old glue cleaned out and reglued so the joint holds again.

Treated at the finish where possible; a deeper mark may mean refinishing the top.

Repair, refinish or leave it alone?

Not every older piece should be stripped. An original finish, even a worn one, is part of what an antique is worth, and once it is sanded away it cannot be put back. For a family piece or something with real age, cleaning, regluing and touching up the existing finish is often the better job. Our antique furniture restoration page explains how we decide.

For everyday furniture that has simply worn out its finish, a full refinish makes sense: old finish off, repairs done, new stain and topcoat on. Kitchen and dining tables take the hardest use, so table refinishing has its own page.

East Texas humidity plays a part in a lot of what we see. Wood takes on moisture in the damp months and gives it up when the air conditioning runs, and that constant movement is why glue joints let go and veneer lifts. A repair that accounts for that movement lasts.

Common questions

Is there a furniture repair shop near me in East Texas?

Grace Woodwork is a furniture repair shop in Kilgore, in Gregg County. We take work from Longview, Tyler, Henderson, Marshall, Gladewater, White Oak and the other towns listed on this page, and we pick up and deliver pieces that do not fit in a car.

What kinds of wood furniture can you repair?

Dining tables and chairs, dressers, chests, beds, desks, china cabinets, armoires, rocking chairs and church pews are typical pieces we work on. Our work is the wood: frames, joints and finishes. Upholstery, leather and recliner mechanisms are not on our list of services.

Is it worth repairing old furniture instead of buying new?

Often, yes. An older solid wood piece with a loose joint or a worn top usually needs a repair, not a replacement. When the damage is in particleboard, or the repair would cost more than the piece is worth to you, we say so before you spend anything.

What is the difference between furniture repair, refinishing and restoration?

Repair fixes the structure: joints, legs, drawers, veneer. Refinishing replaces the finish. Restoration is the careful version of both for an older or family piece, where we keep as much of the original wood and finish as we can. Many jobs are a mix of the three.

How much does furniture repair cost?

It depends on the piece and what has failed, so we price each job after seeing a photo or the piece itself. Our furniture refinishing cost guide lists published national price ranges and what moves a price up or down.
