Repair beats replacement, and it is not close

On anything built before about 1980, the piece you already own is better made than what the same money would buy new. Solid timber instead of particle board, joints that were cut instead of stapled, and a frame that can be taken apart and put back together rather than thrown out when one corner goes.

A dining chair that has started to rock does not need a new set. It needs taking apart, the old glue cleaned out, and gluing and clamping properly. Done right it will outlast anything you would have bought to replace it, and it costs a fraction of the price.

A railroad town from the start

Big Sandy began as Big Sandy Switch, where the Texas and Pacific line met the narrow-gauge Tyler Tap — both part of the Union Pacific now. The post office, a newspaper, stores and churches followed, and the settlement incorporated in 1926 with about eight hundred and fifty people. It sits at the junction of Highway 80 and State Highway 155, fourteen miles south-west of Gilmer.

Towns that old have furniture that old, often several generations into the same family. Those pieces are worth keeping and worth doing properly.

What comes in

Loose and broken joints. Chairs that rock or have given way at the back. Drawers that bind, drop or sit crooked. Lifted and blistered veneer. Water rings, heat marks and sun-faded tops. Split boards and cracked legs. Missing mouldings and turned parts cut to match rather than ordered. Pieces somebody already tried to fix once with the wrong glue, which is nothing to apologise for.
