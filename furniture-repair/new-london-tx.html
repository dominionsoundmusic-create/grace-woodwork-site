Furniture Repair in New London, Texas

New London grew on the oilfield, and a town that carries the memory of the 1937 school explosion with a cenotaph that still stands at its centre. It is twenty minutes, so it is an easy run out and back in a day. The houses here are modest, settled homes, many in the same families for generations, and the pieces that come from New London are usually pieces with real history in them, kept because of who owned them rather than what they are worth.

Some jobs are not about the furniture. When a piece is the last thing left from a house or a person, the repair gets the care that deserves.

Why furniture round here needs the work

East Texas humidity is hard on furniture. Joints that were tight in a dry winter work loose by August, and a finish that has never been maintained will cloud. Most of what comes in from New London is that, rather than damage — and it is fixable.

What furniture repair covers

For New London customers that means loose joints, broken chair legs, drawers that stick, veneer lifting at the edges, water rings and heat marks. Repair is almost always cheaper than replacing, and on anything built before about 1980 the piece you own is better made than what you would buy to replace it.

Questions from New London customers

How long does it take?

Depends on the job and what is already in the shop. You will be told a realistic time up front, not an optimistic one.

Is it worth repairing or should I replace it?

Sometimes replacing is the right answer and you will be told so. Anything built before about 1980 is usually worth keeping — it was made better than what would replace it.

Do you come out to New London?

Yes. New London is twenty minutes from the shop in Kilgore, and pickup and delivery can be arranged for anything too big to move yourself.

How much will it cost?

It depends entirely on the piece, which is why nothing is quoted sight unseen. Send a photo and you will get a real number before any work starts.

Refinishing is not always the answer

Stripping a piece back is sometimes exactly wrong. An original finish, even a tired one, is part of what an older piece is worth, and once it is off it does not come back. Cleaning and reviving what is there is often the better job and the cheaper one.
