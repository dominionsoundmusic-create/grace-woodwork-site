Fixing Furniture Veneer: What You Can Do and When to Call

Lifted edges, bubbles in the middle of a top, chips along a drawer front. Veneer damage looks alarming, but it is one of the most repairable problems in old furniture, as long as nobody makes it worse first.

What veneer is

Veneer is a thin sheet of wood glued over a solid base. It lets a cabinetmaker use beautiful figured wood across a whole dresser top without the top warping, and it has been used on fine furniture for centuries.

The weak point is the glue line. Most older veneered furniture was assembled with hide glue, which softens with heat and moisture. Over decades of humid East Texas summers and dry, air-conditioned winters, it lets go in patches: an edge lifts, a bubble rises in the middle of a top, or a corner snaps off and disappears.

The Conservation Center lists long exposure to heat and humidity, including heat registers and sunny windows, as a common cause of lifted edges, blisters and cracks.

What you can safely do right now

  • Save every loose piece. Put chips and strips in an envelope taped inside a drawer. Original veneer matches better than any patch.
  • Do not pull or trim it. A lifted edge that is tugged or cut off turns a reglue into a patch.
  • Keep it from snagging. Lay a soft cloth or a sheet of paper over the area and avoid sliding things across it.
  • Avoid tape on the finish. Most tapes pull finish, and sometimes veneer, off when removed.
  • Keep it dry and away from heat. Move the piece off heating and cooling vents and out of direct sun.

This is shop guidance from our own work rather than a published standard, and it is the same thing we would tell a neighbor. The aim is simply to stop the damage spreading until the piece can be repaired.

The American Institute for Conservation recommends keeping furniture where relative humidity stays roughly between 40 and 60 percent and away from vents and heaters. That is the long-term fix that keeps new lifts from starting.

How a professional veneer repair is done

Regluing lifted veneer. The old glue under the loose area is cleaned out or softened so new glue can bond. At The Henry Ford, conservators softened old glue with warm water, scraped it out, laid in fresh hot hide glue and clamped the veneer flat. A conservator at the University of Delaware injected hide glue under lifted veneer or reactivated the original glue with a little water and warmth.

Clamping evenly. Woodcraft notes that the clamping block, or caul, has to cover the whole loose area or the gaps turn into bubbles, with wax paper between the caul and the veneer, and about a day to cure.

Patching missing veneer. A new piece is cut from veneer matched for species, color and grain direction, shaped to the hole, glued in, leveled and colored to blend with the finish.

Finish touch-up. The repaired area is blended into the surrounding finish so it does not show.

When to bring it to a shop

Call a professional when: the piece is old, valuable or a family heirloom; the loose area is larger than your palm or in the middle of a top; pieces are missing; the veneer has bubbled in several places; or the surface also has water damage. Heat guns, irons and household glues used without experience can scorch veneer or leave glue that makes a proper repair harder.

Common questions

How do you repair furniture veneer?

Lifted or bubbled veneer is reglued: the old glue under it is cleaned or softened, fresh glue goes in, and the veneer is clamped flat under a padded block until it cures. Missing pieces are patched with veneer matched for species, color and grain. Museum conservators often use hide glue, which bonds to the old glue and can be undone later.

Why does veneer lift or bubble?

Older furniture was usually veneered with hide glue, which weakens with repeated humidity swings, heat and water. The veneer and the wood beneath it also move at different rates. Conservators at The Henry Ford traced dozens of lifted areas on one chest to failed hide glue.

Can I glue veneer back down myself?

A small lifted edge on an everyday piece can be, if you can get glue under it and clamp it evenly. On anything valuable, old or with a large loose area, a shop repair is safer, because the wrong glue or uneven pressure makes the next repair much harder.

How can I tell veneer from solid wood?

Look at an edge or the corner of a drawer front. Solid wood shows its grain running around the corner into end grain; veneer shows a thin seam or a separate edge band. The grain inside a drawer front may also differ from the outside.

Is veneered furniture worth repairing?

Often, yes. Veneer is not a sign of cheap furniture: fine furniture has been veneered for centuries. What matters is the condition of the wood under it and whether the piece matters to you.
