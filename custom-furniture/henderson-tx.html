Furnishing a house that is older than the furniture industry

Henderson has houses from the 1880s and earlier, and nineteen historical markers to go with them. Rooms in those houses were laid out before anybody was manufacturing furniture to standard dimensions. Ceilings are higher. Doorways are narrower. Fireplaces sit where a modern floor plan would never put them, and the alcoves either side are never the same width as each other.

Shop-bought furniture in a house like that always looks slightly wrong, and usually is. Building to the room is not a luxury in those houses, it is the only way to get something that fits and looks like it belongs.

Proportion matters more than period detail

People often ask for a reproduction and what they actually need is the right proportion. A plain, well-made table at the correct height and length will sit comfortably in a Victorian room, where a piece covered in applied ornament usually will not, the ornament reads as new no matter how carefully it is done.

So the conversation is usually about scale, timber and finish rather than about copying a style. We will tell you honestly when a reproduction detail is going to look like a reproduction.

What gets built

Dining and farmhouse tables at whatever length the room takes. Benches, including the stepped kind that suit a hall or a porch. Bed frames, often to fit an older room where a modern frame will not clear the door. Console and sofa tables sized to the wall. Bookcases and shelving run to the real ceiling height rather than stopping short. Coffee tables. Blanket chests. Children's step stools. Plant stands. Kitchen islands.

There is a crew here, not one pair of hands

Grace Woodwork is several people, which matters when a job is more than one piece. A set of six chairs does not take six times as long as one, and a room's worth of furniture does not sit waiting on a single person to work through it in order.

How a build starts

With a measurement and an idea, plus a photograph of the room it is going in, that matters more in an old house than anywhere else, because the room is doing half the deciding. From there we talk timber, finish, price and timing, and nothing is cut until you have a real number. Henderson is about twenty-five minutes from the shop, and we install rather than leaving it in the driveway.

Questions we get from Henderson

Can you build something to suit an older house?

Yes, and usually by getting the proportion right rather than by adding period ornament. We will tell you when a reproduction detail would read as new.

Can you match a piece I already own?

Often. Bring a photo or the piece itself. An exact match to an aged finish is not something to promise, but we will get close and say how close.

Do you deliver to Henderson?

Yes, and we install. It is about twenty-five minutes from the Kilgore shop.

What timber do you use?

Pine, oak and cedar most often, and reclaimed timber when a piece calls for it.
