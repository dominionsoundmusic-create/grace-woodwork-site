Country houses, and what they actually need

A lot of Upshur County is rural, and rural houses have needs that suburban furniture catalogs never address. A table long enough for a family that still eats together on Sundays. Bench seating rather than chairs, because benches take more people and slide under. Storage for a mud room that is genuinely muddy. Shelving in a barn or a shop that will hold weight rather than sag in a year.

Built to measure is not a luxury purchase in that context. It is often the only way to get something the right size that will actually stand up to use, and it usually costs less than the equivalent bought new and delivered.

The Yamboree and the tables it needs

Gilmer takes in something over a hundred thousand visitors across four days on the third weekend of October for the East Texas Yamboree, which has run since 1935 and has only been cancelled twice, for the war and for the pandemic. A town of about five thousand hosts twenty times its own population.

That means trestle tables, market stalls, display stands and booth fittings, and it means anyone selling there has one weekend where their display either works or does not. Those get built well before October, because the week before the Yamboree is not when anybody in this county has spare time.

Church and hall furniture

Small-town East Texas runs a great deal of its life through its churches, and their buildings need furniture nobody sells in a shop, communion tables, lecterns, pew repairs and extensions, tables for the fellowship hall that seat properly and store flat. We do that work, and we understand it usually comes out of a committee budget rather than a household one, so it gets quoted plainly and honestly.

There is a crew here, not one pair of hands

Grace Woodwork is several people, which matters when the job is a set rather than a piece. Eight matching hall tables do not take eight times as long as one, and a job with a fixed date on it does not sit waiting on a single person to work through the list in order.

How to start

With a measurement and an idea, and a photograph of the space if there is one. From there we talk timber, finish, price and timing, and nothing is cut until you have a real number. Gilmer is about thirty-five minutes from the shop and we deliver and install.

Questions we get from Gilmer

Do you deliver to Gilmer?

Yes, and we install. It is about thirty-five minutes from the Kilgore shop.

Can you build stalls or display tables for the Yamboree?

Yes, and order them well before October rather than the week before, when nobody in the county has spare time.

Do you do church and hall furniture?

Yes. Communion tables, lecterns, pew repairs and fellowship hall tables, quoted plainly.

What timber do you use?

Pine, oak and cedar most often, and reclaimed timber when a piece calls for it.
