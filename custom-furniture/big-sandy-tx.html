Plain and solid beats fancy and fragile

Not everything needs to be elaborate. A great deal of the best furniture ever made in East Texas was plain — a straight table in solid timber, a bench that seats five, shelving that holds what you put on it. Made properly, those pieces last generations and cost less than the ornate version.

That is most of what we build for this area, and it is not a compromise. Simple joinery done well outlasts complicated joinery done quickly, and it is easier to repair in twenty years' time.

Built so it can be fixed

Everything we make is put together so it can be taken apart and repaired rather than thrown away. A joint that can be re-glued is the difference between a piece that lasts a decade and one that lasts a lifetime. That is worth more than any amount of decoration.

What gets built

Dining and farmhouse tables. Benches, which seat more people than chairs and cost less. Bed frames. Blanket chests. Bookcases and shelving. Coffee tables. Children's step stools. Plant stands. Kitchen islands. Feed, tack and workshop storage. Church and hall furniture, which comes up steadily in a town this size.

Say what your budget is

It genuinely helps to tell us at the start. There is usually more than one way to build a thing, and knowing the number lets us pick the version that fits it — a different timber, a simpler leg, an oil finish instead of a lacquered one. That is a better conversation than being quoted something you were never going to spend.

How a build starts

With a measurement and an idea, and a photograph of the space if there is one. Then timber, finish, price and timing, and nothing is cut until you have a real number. Big Sandy is about thirty minutes from the shop and we deliver and install.
