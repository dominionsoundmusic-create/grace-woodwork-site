Cabinet refacing in one paragraph

Refacing means keeping your cabinet boxes and replacing everything you see on the front of them. The doors and drawer fronts are new, the hinges and pulls are new, and the face frames and exposed sides are covered so they match. Inside, the cabinets are the same ones you had.

Because the boxes stay, so do the plumbing, the countertops and the layout. That is both the appeal and the limit: refacing changes how a kitchen looks, not how it works. If you need more storage, a different layout or an island, that is a new-cabinet job.

Other guides publish wider ranges for refacing: Fixr about $3,000 to $13,500, Forbes Home about $1,350 to $12,500, and Angi a typical $4,484 to $10,586. Material is the biggest swing: HomeGuide lists laminate as the least expensive, wood veneer in the middle and solid wood the most.

Is your kitchen a refacing kitchen?

Refacing only works when the boxes are worth keeping. HomeGuide notes the boxes need to be structurally sound, because refacing only covers the outside. Signs that they are:

  • Boxes are solid, square and dry, with no swollen particleboard under the sink
  • You like the layout and the countertops
  • The door style is what dates the kitchen, or the doors are damaged

Signs that they are not: water damage in the boxes, cabinets pulling away from the wall, or a layout you would change if you could. Angi also notes that when a thermofoil finish is splitting, refacing is a better fit than refinishing, because the damaged doors are replaced instead of patched.

Materials used for refacing

Wood veneer is a thin layer of real wood glued to the frames, with solid wood or veneered doors to match. It can be stained or painted. HomeGuide describes it as more durable than laminate.

Plastic laminate is the least expensive facing and comes in many colors and wood looks. It can chip on impact, and later it can only be painted, not stained.

Rigid thermofoil is a vinyl skin heat-pressed over fiberboard doors. Angi says it lasts about 10 to 15 years with care and can blister or peel near ovens and dishwashers without heat shields.
