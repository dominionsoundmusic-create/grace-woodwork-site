When the boxes are good and the doors are the problem

A lot of East Texas kitchens have cabinet boxes that will outlast the house and doors that went out of style decades ago: raised-panel oak, cathedral arches, worn thermofoil. Refacing fixes what you see without paying to replace what you do not.

On a refacing job we build new doors and drawer fronts in the Kilgore shop to the size of each opening. The face frames and the visible end panels are covered so they match the new doors, the hinges and pulls are replaced, and the old fronts go away. The cabinet boxes, the plumbing, the countertops and the layout all stay as they are.

That is what separates refacing from cabinet painting, which keeps the old doors and changes their color, and from new custom cabinets, which replaces everything. If you want to compare all three in detail, read what cabinet refacing is.

What changes and what stays

New

Doors, drawer fronts, the facing on the frames and exposed sides, hinges and pulls.

Kept

Cabinet boxes, shelves, countertops, sink and appliances, and the layout of the room.

Optional

New drawer boxes or soft-close slides, a few added cabinets, crown molding, a new pantry door to match.

Not possible

Moving cabinets or changing the layout. That is a new-cabinet job.

Door styles and finishes

Shaker doors, with a flat center panel inside a square frame, suit both older and newer houses. Slab doors give a cleaner, modern look. Doors can be painted any color, or built in a wood that takes stain so the grain shows.

The facing on the frames has to match the doors. On a painted job that is simple; on a stained job the species and the stain are matched so the frames and doors read as one piece of furniture.

Facing materials vary between companies. Wood veneer, plastic laminate and rigid thermofoil are all sold for refacing; published guides describe wood veneer as more durable than laminate, and thermofoil as prone to blistering near heat. Ask us what goes on your frames before you agree to anything, and we will tell you.
