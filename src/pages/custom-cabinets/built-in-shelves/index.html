Shelves that look like part of the house

A built-in is a piece of furniture that belongs to one wall. It runs to the ceiling or the window sill, it is trimmed to the room, and it does not wobble or leave a dusty gap behind it the way a store bookcase does.

Built-in bookshelves often go beside a fireplace, around a TV, under a staircase or along a home office wall with a desk worked in. Closets, pantries, laundry rooms, mudrooms and garages get the same treatment in a tougher painted finish.

Older East Texas houses are good candidates because their walls are rarely straight and their alcoves are rarely a standard size. Building to the actual opening, rather than buying the nearest size and filling the gap, is what makes a built-in look original to the house.

Garage

Sturdy shelving and cabinets for tools and storage.

Planning a built-in

Start with what will live on the shelves. Paperbacks, record albums, display pieces and baskets all want different shelf depths and spacing, and the shelves should be adjustable where it makes sense so the unit still works when your things change.

Then look at the wall. We measure the opening at several heights, check for outlets, switches, vents and windows, and find the studs so the unit can be fastened properly. A long shelf that carries books needs support at shorter spans than a shelf of light decor, or it will sag over time.

Finally, the finish. A painted built-in matched to the trim reads as part of the house; a stained one reads as furniture. Either way it is finished in the shop and touched up after it is installed.

If the room also needs new kitchen or bathroom cabinets, see custom cabinets. A freestanding bookcase or desk is on custom furniture.
