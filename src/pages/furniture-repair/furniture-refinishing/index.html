What furniture refinishing is

Refinishing means replacing the finish: the old coating is removed down to bare wood, the surface is prepared, and a new stain and protective finish are built up in coats. West Virginia University Extension describes it the same way, and separates it from restoration, which keeps the original finish.

It is the right call when the finish is past saving: worn through on the arms and edges, crazed and flaking, blackened by water that got into the wood, or simply a color that no longer suits the house. It is the wrong call when a cleaning and a few touch-ups would bring the old finish back, which is why we look first.

Dressers, chests, dining tables and chairs, china cabinets and hutches, armoires, desks, rocking chairs and church pews all come through our shop for refinishing. The gallery shows refinished pieces and a few caught halfway through stripping.

Revive, refinish or paint?

ApproachWhat happensBest for
Clean and reviveGrime and old wax removed, scratches and dull spots treated, finish left onSound finishes that are just tired; antiques with original finish
Full refinishOld finish removed, repairs done, new stain and topcoatFinishes that are worn through, flaking or water-damaged
PaintSurface cleaned, sanded and primed, then paintedA big color change, or wood that is patched or mismatched

Fine Woodworking calls stripping the "nuclear option" and recommends reviving any finish that is still in reasonable shape. We agree, and we will tell you when your piece only needs the lighter treatment.

How we refinish a piece

  1. Find out what the finish isShellac, lacquer, varnish and oil finishes each behave differently. Knowing which is on the piece decides how it comes off and what can go back on.
  2. Repairs firstLoose joints reglued, veneer laid back down, chips filled and missing parts made before any new finish goes on.
  3. Remove the old finishTaken off carefully so carvings, edges and thin veneer are not damaged.
  4. Prepare the woodSanded smooth, stains and marks dealt with, and the grain prepared for color.
  5. Color and finishStain matched to your sample or your room, then a protective topcoat built up in thin coats.

Keeping a new finish looking new

Wood keeps moving after it is refinished. The American Institute for Conservation advises keeping furniture where relative humidity stays roughly between 40 and 60 percent, away from heating and cooling vents and out of direct sun. In an East Texas house that swings from damp spring to dry air conditioning, that means not parking a dresser on top of a floor register.

Day to day, dust with a soft cloth, use coasters and trivets, and wipe spills instead of letting them sit. If a ring or a cloudy patch appears, call before trying a home remedy; many marks are in the finish rather than the wood and are easier to fix early.
