Chairs take the hardest beating in the house

Every time someone tips back, drags a chair out from the table or sits down hard, the joints take the strain. Over years the glue lets go, the wood around the joint wears, and the chair starts to rock. It is an ordinary repair, and a very fixable one.

We repair wooden dining chairs, kitchen chairs, rocking chairs, ladder-backs, desk chairs with wood frames, and chairs with woven seats. The repair depends on what failed: a joint, a part, or the seat itself.

Upholstered seats and recliner mechanisms are outside what we do. If your chair has a wood frame with a cane or solid wood seat, it is the kind we work on.

Why screws and glue in the gap do not last

The quick fixes for a wobbly chair are a screw through the joint, a nail, or glue squeezed into the gap. They feel solid for a while. Woodworker's Journal explains why they fail: new glue does not bond well to old glue, and the only way to restore a joint's strength is to take it apart, clean off the old glue, then reglue and clamp it.

Screws and nails add holes in exactly the place the wood is already stressed. When the joint loosens again, it takes more wood with it.

The right repair is slower:

  • The chair is taken apart at the loose joints, gently, so nothing splits
  • Old glue is cleaned off the tenons, dowels and holes
  • Worn joints are built back up with new wood where needed
  • The chair is reglued and clamped square and level, then left to cure

Conservators note that not every joint should be forced apart, especially where pegs or old nails hold it. Part of the job is knowing which ones to leave.

Cane chair repair

Cane seats wear through in the middle first, where people sit. There are two main kinds, and the repair is different for each.

Hand caning. If you see a row of small holes drilled around the seat frame, the seat was woven by hand, strand by strand, usually in the traditional seven-step pattern. A worn hand-caned seat is cut out and rewoven through the same holes.

Pressed or sheet cane. If there is a groove around the seat opening with a strip of reed (the spline) pressed into it, the cane was woven on a loom and set in as one sheet. The repair is to remove the old spline and sheet, clean the groove, and set in a new sheet and spline.

Rush seats, made from twisted cattail leaves or a paper fiber substitute, and woven splint seats are a different craft; send a photo and we will tell you whether yours is a job we can take on. Our guide how chair caning is repaired explains each kind in more detail.

Other chair repairs

Broken legs and rungs

Glued and reinforced, or remade from matching wood when the old part is too far gone.

Split seats and backs

Cracked solid seats and broken back slats or spindles reglued or replaced.

Rocking chairs

Loose rockers, arms and spindles, and worn finish on the arms where hands rest.

Matching a set

Chairs refinished alongside the table so the set matches. See table refinishing.
