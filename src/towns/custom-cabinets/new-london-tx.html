Ask first whether you need new cabinets

A good many kitchens do not need replacing. Where the carcasses are solid and sound, it is the doors, drawer runners, handles and finish that have had their day, and new fronts with proper hardware and a refinish gets a different kitchen for a fraction of a rebuild.

Where the carcass material itself has gone, and once water is into particle board, it has, new fronts on a failing box is money thrown away. Telling those apart takes five minutes standing in the kitchen, and we will say plainly which you have, including when it is the cheap answer.

Built to fit

Factory cabinets come in fixed widths in three-inch steps and assume square walls and standard heights. Older houses do not oblige, and you end up with filler strips, a corner nobody can reach into and inches of wall doing nothing. Building to the actual opening removes all of it.

What we build uses plywood carcasses rather than particle board, jointed drawer boxes rather than stapled ones, and runners rated for real loads.

A room at a time is fine

There is no requirement to do everything at once. Plenty of jobs get done in stages, the failing run this year, the pantry next. Tell us that is the plan and it gets built so the later stages match the earlier ones rather than looking like an add-on.
