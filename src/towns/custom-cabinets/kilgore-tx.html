Stock cabinets and old Kilgore kitchens do not agree

Cabinets come from a factory in fixed widths, in three-inch steps. Kitchens in houses built during and after the oil boom do not. The result is the thing everyone recognises: filler strips down the side of the run, a corner nobody can reach into, and eight inches of wasted wall because the next size up would not go in.

Built-to-fit cabinetry starts from the opening you actually have. The run goes wall to wall, the corner gets a proper carousel or a deep drawer, and the awkward space beside the chimney breast becomes a cupboard instead of a gap.

Keep the boxes, change everything else

A lot of the kitchens we look at round here do not need tearing out. In houses of this age the carcasses are often solid timber and perfectly sound, and it is the doors, the drawer runners, the handles and the finish that have had their day. Replacing those, new doors and drawer fronts, new hardware, the whole run painted or refinished, gets you a different kitchen for a fraction of a rebuild.

We will tell you which of the two your kitchen needs after looking at it, and we will say so if the honest answer is the cheaper one.

Downtown Kilgore work

Historic downtown Kilgore has kept its Art Deco frontages from the boom years and now runs on independent shops and restaurants under the Texas Main Street program. Fitting out a space like that is a different job from a domestic kitchen, counters, display, back-of-house storage, and doing it in a way that suits a period building rather than fighting it. We take that work.

How a cabinet job starts

With a look at the room. Photographs get the conversation going, but cabinetry gets measured in person, walls in houses this old are rarely square, and that has to be built in rather than discovered on fitting day. You will get a real price before anything is cut.
