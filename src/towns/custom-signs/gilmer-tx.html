Farm and gate signs

This is the sign work that comes up most in Upshur County and least everywhere else. A place name on a gate, a family name at the end of a drive, an established date, a brand. Those go up once and stay up for decades, in full sun and full weather, usually a long way from anywhere anyone can maintain them.

Which means construction matters more than decoration. Timber chosen for outdoors, joints cut rather than stapled, lettering with real depth so it still reads when the surface has weathered, and a finish that will take an East Texas summer. Tell us how exposed the spot is and how far from the house it sits, and it gets built for that rather than for a porch.

The Yamboree, and ordering in good time

Gilmer takes in over a hundred thousand visitors on the third weekend of October for the East Texas Yamboree, which has run since 1935. Anyone trading that weekend, a stall, a shop on the square, a booth, is showing their frontage to twenty times the town's population, most of whom have never seen it before.

Practical advice: order signage months ahead. September is when everybody in Gilmer remembers at once, and a rushed exterior sign is a badly finished one, the finish on outdoor work needs time to cure properly if it is going to last.

Made in a furniture shop

These are built as timber pieces, real frames, real boards, joints cut rather than stapled, and a finish put on the way a table gets finished. That is why they survive on a gatepost, and why they sit properly next to good furniture indoors instead of looking like gift shop stock.

Church, family and house signs

Family names and established dates. House numbers. Scripture, which is asked for more in this county than anywhere else we work. Church signs and noticeboards. Nursery and children's room pieces. Wedding and anniversary signs. Memorial pieces for a family place. One sign for one room is a perfectly normal order.

Coat racks and hooks

A close cousin of the sign work and one of the most-asked-for things in the shop. A stained board with cast hooks, cut to the length of wall you actually have, sorts out a back door or a mud room that never had anywhere to hang anything. They take lettering too, which makes them a sign and a coat rack at once.

Ordering from Gilmer

Send the wording, the rough size, and a photograph of where it is going, a gatepost in open sun and a shopfront under an awning need different construction, not just different paint. You get a price before anything is cut, and Gilmer is about thirty-five minutes from the shop.
