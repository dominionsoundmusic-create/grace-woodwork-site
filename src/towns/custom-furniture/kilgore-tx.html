Old houses have awkward rooms

Kilgore's housing mostly went up between the 1930s boom and the 1960s, and houses from that stretch were not built around today's furniture. Dining rooms are narrower. Hallways are tighter. Bedrooms in the boom-era houses were sized for a different kind of life. Anyone who has tried to fit a shop-bought eight-seater into a 1940s dining room already knows the problem.

Building to the room solves it in a way that shopping around never quite does. A table nine inches shorter than any standard size, a bench that fits the bay, shelving that runs to the actual ceiling height rather than stopping awkwardly short.

What gets built here

Dining tables and farmhouse tables. Benches, including the stepped and staggered ones that suit a hallway or a porch. Bed frames. Console and sofa tables. Bookcases and open shelving. Coffee tables. Step stools for children. Plant stands. Blanket chests. Kitchen islands. If it is made of wood and it needs to fit a particular space, it is worth asking about.

How a build starts

With a measurement and an idea. A photograph of something similar helps, even if it is off the internet, it says more about what you actually want than a description does. From there we will talk about wood, finish, what it will cost and how long it will take, and none of it starts until you have a real number.

Where we build and where we deliver

Everything is made in the Kilgore shop. Delivery and installation across East Texas, Longview and Gladewater are a few minutes, and we have taken work as far as Lufkin when the job justified the drive. If you are further out than that, ask anyway.
